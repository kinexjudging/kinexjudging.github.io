The problem

Right now that mark travels by hand signal.

A judge holds up fingers, or hollers it across the arena, or writes it on a card that walks to the booth between sections. The secretary hears it wrong, the announcer reads a number that changes ten minutes later, and the video board is always a ride behind. None of that is anybody's fault — there has never been a way to send it.

Kinex is that way. Nothing about how your judges mark changes. What changes is that the number arrives.

How it works

Three screens, one number.

A mark makes one trip. These are the three places it shows up, in the order it gets there.

FIRST

The judge's phone

Whoever the office has loaded is already on screen — name, hometown, animal, contractor. The judge taps one mark on a keypad, decimals and all, and sends it. No paperwork, no scrolling to find the right rider.

THEN

The office board

The secretary sees each judge's mark land as it comes in, and the total the moment the last one is there. Every event has its own tab, with the field, the rerides and a live leaderboard underneath.

AND

The video board

The contestant, the score, the chute clock and the leaderboard go straight to vMix — so the jumbotron shows the same number the secretary is looking at, without anyone typing it twice.

Built for the way rodeos actually run

The parts that only matter once you're at one.

No signal needed

Arena wifi will drop. It doesn't matter.

Every phone holds the whole day sheet. When the signal goes, judges keep marking — the marks queue up on the phone with the time they were made and send themselves the moment it comes back. A mark is never lost, and a late one never overwrites a correction.

Chute clock

For the ones taking too long.

Set it anywhere from 15 to 60 seconds. A judge starts it from the floor and it appears at once on the secretary's board, the announcer's screen and the video board — the same count on every one. It stops at zero rather than running on.

Rerides

Awarded, parked, and brought back.

A reride moves the contestant into their own section on that event with the reason attached. When they nod on the reride animal, one press puts them back in the chute for a score or a no-score like anybody else.

Called from the floor

Buck-offs, no-scores, turn-outs.

A judge raises it from their phone and it lands on the office board with their name on it. The secretary commits it. Two judges don't always agree, and what's happening behind the chutes isn't visible from the arena floor — so the office has the last word.

When a phone dies

The secretary can key it in.

Any judge's mark can be entered from the office, and it's recorded as having come from the office rather than the phone. A dead battery in the middle of the bull riding does not stop the rodeo.

Afterwards

The full sheet, downloaded.

When the event is over, pull the leaderboard as a spreadsheet with every judge's own mark beside the total — not just the score. Everyone entered is on it, including the turn-outs and the no-scores.

The ride clock

Eight seconds, and the horn.

A judge starts the ride clock from the phone when the gate opens; it runs on every screen and the horn sounds from the office laptop, through your sound board, on the second. Freestyle bullfighting counts down with a warning horn. The office can always start and stop it from the board.

Bull teams

Riders and bulls, scored apart.

The bull team event is built in: each judge types the bull and the rider into their own box, to the hundredth, one judge keys the ride time, and a rider who bucks off is a zero while the bull still scores for its team. Riders and teams are ranked as you go.

Timed events

Off your arena timer, penalties by name.

Hook up the FarmTek and the time shows live on the board and lands on its own. A barrel down, a broken barrier, a leg: one press each, added to the time, and printed on the results. Day sheets come in from Kinex Entry as a file, and results go back the same way.

The video board

It already speaks vMix.

Three ways in, because no two production trucks are set up the same. Use whichever fits how you already build your show.

1

Browser overlay

Point a vMix browser input at a URL. Transparent background, nothing to configure, on air in a minute.

2

Data sources

Feed your own titles from XML or JSON, so the graphics stay in your house style.

3

Pushed on the beat

Kinex drives vMix directly over its API — sets the text and pulls the overlay in the moment the score lands.

Owning it

Licensed to your outfit.

▸

The key works offline. It never phones home to be checked. If the venue has no internet at all, the rodeo runs exactly the same.
